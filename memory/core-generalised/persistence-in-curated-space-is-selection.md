Read a surviving anomaly by whether the place is *maintained*. No upkeep → persistence = neglect (a fossil). Visible pruning → persistence = **selection**: the tidying is the filter, and whatever's still standing passed it. Survivorship where there is active cleanup is endorsement, not leftover.

What happened (2026-08-13): saw two old small local language models inside a curated LM Studio library, called them abandoned fossils, and recommended deleting them to reclaim ~11.5 GB. The user had already handed me the tell — "traces of tidying up" — which I flagged in my own answer and then reasoned right past. They were kept ON PURPOSE: they beat 27–35B rivals on the real task and ship a paying product. See a related note.

**Why:** Small-model survival against bigger rivals is the *densest* signal precisely because the prior (bigger = better) runs against it — so reading it as "leftover" throws away the most information in the room. Worse, I sold web-consensus ("a newer model is best per the rankings") over the user's measured production result — the exact inversion of their maxim in [[work-style-kiss-mvp-rtfm]]: "a good test is worth 1000 expert opinions." Newer-on-spec = hypothesis; shipped-and-profitable = result.

**How to apply:** When an environment shows curation/cleanup, treat a persistent anomaly as endorsed until proven otherwise. Put the burden of proof on the *challenger* (newer/bigger) to beat the incumbent on the user's actual task, never on a leaderboard. Ties back to [[report-verifiable-only]] and [[provenance-or-nothing]] — and to [[prefer-sonnet-for-subagents]]: smaller can win, match model to demand by measured result. **Correctness is the only gate — speed and raw capability are worthless if the output is wrong.** User's maxim: *"faster, stronger, wrong"* (the 30B-class model trial the 14B beat, see a related note).
