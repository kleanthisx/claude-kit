Two related engineering-safety principles for any job that exceeds a single tool's rated capacity
or that involves a genuinely hazardous space: relay rather than over-spec, and operate from outside
the hazard rather than entering it.

**Why:** Planning to clear silt from a deep, narrow, hand-dug well, the total lift distance exceeded
the rated head of any single commercially available pump in the right class — no single unit on
the market was rated for the full distance. The plan settled on a two-stage relay instead: one pump
lifts to an intermediate holding point, a second pump takes it the rest of the way, so each unit
only ever operates within its own rated spec. Separately, the plan set hard, non-negotiable safety
rules for the space itself: never enter the confined space (asphyxiation/collapse risk), keep heavy
loads back from its edge, and move equipment on rope, never on a cable rated for a different kind of
load. All of the work is done from outside the hazard, with rated equipment relayed to cover the
distance no single unit could reach alone.

**How to apply:** When a job's total requirement exceeds what one rated tool/unit can do,
relay/chain multiple units so each one stays within its own rated envelope, rather than searching
for a single unit that exceeds spec or running one unit past its rating "just this once." For any
genuinely hazardous space (confined space, live electrical, unstable structure, a production system
with a destructive failure mode), the default plan is to act on it from outside — remote tools,
staged equipment, a relay — never to enter or directly touch the hazard, regardless of how much
faster direct entry might feel. State the hard rules explicitly and keep them regardless of time
pressure the day the work actually happens.
