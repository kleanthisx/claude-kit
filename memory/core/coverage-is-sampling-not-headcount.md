*(Written as requirements, not prohibitions — see [[instruction-decay-evidence]].)*

**Report the sample count, the map, and where the map came from.** 5/5 agents and 5/125 agents are the same thing — 5 samples of an unknown space. 5/5 is the more dangerous of the two, because the hole doesn't show.

Coverage is a property of the DOMAIN, not of the pool I launched. **Earn a coverage claim one of exactly two ways:**

1. **An externally-sourced denominator.** A published taxonomy, standards register or index somebody else maintains. *"There are 8 national food databases in the EU; we read 8"* is a denominator with provenance. *"12 agents"* is a budget wearing one.
2. **Observed saturation.** Keep sampling until fresh samples return only content already seen. Repetition is the only empirical evidence of coverage there is, and it needs volume — at ~125 samples you cover the body of the curve; at 5 you may sit entirely in one corner and be unable to tell.

- **Overlap agent assignments deliberately**, so repeats can appear. Redundancy is the measuring instrument; disjoint lanes destroy the only coverage signal available.
- **Aim explicitly at the tails.** Volume buys the body of the distribution; tails have to be targeted.
- **State which of the two mechanisms is in play before any fan-out.** If neither, call the work something other than research.

**Why:** 2026-08-11 — I launched 12 agents across 12 disjoint lanes and would have called whatever returned a research report. User: "it's both just 5 samples… of the unknown", and "at least with 125 chances are you cover 80% of the bell curve."

**How to apply:** Give counts and maps, not ratios. Name the coverage mechanism up front. See [[provenance-or-nothing]], [[research-method-rules]], [[w-questions-before-actions]].
